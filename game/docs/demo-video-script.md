# Demo video — script and shot list

Goal: in about 75 seconds, a stranger understands what kan! is, feels like trying it, and knows
where to get it. Tone: warm and calm, like the game. One main cut (16:9, 1080p60) for YouTube,
Reddit and the README, and a 30-second vertical cut (9:16) for Shorts, Reels and TikTok.

## Legal rules for the video (read before recording)

- **Only free-to-use music on screen and in the audio**, including what plays in the background of
  the editor. Platforms match audio automatically (YouTube Content ID): one copyrighted song can
  mute or block the video. Use a public-domain melody with a companion that is free to use
  (`charts/docs/free-song-candidates.md`), or the player's own playing.
- **No Nintendo material**: no Zelda melodies, names, logos or footage, even though the ocarina
  remake is what people are talking about. The melodies are copyrighted and the names are
  trademarks. Ride the wave with words that are ours ("got an ocarina?") and generic tags
  (`#ocarina`, `#rhythmgame`), never with their songs.
- No osu! material: the game already shows kan!'s own logo, intro and backgrounds.

## Vertical cut (30 s)

0–3 s hands + gold notes ("Your instrument is the controller") → 3–18 s gameplay, full screen,
with the hands in a corner → 18–25 s editor time-lapse ("Any song becomes a level") → 25–30 s
logo + "Free · link in bio". Burn the captions in: most people watch with the sound off.

## How to record

- **Game footage:** OBS, game window capture, 1920×1080, 60 fps; turn off notifications;
  windowed or borderless.
- **Game audio and microphone on separate tracks** (OBS → Advanced Audio Properties → tracks), so
  the instrument can be balanced against the companion while editing.
- **Hands/instrument:** a phone on a stand, 1080p60, good light from the side. Clap once at the
  start of each take to line up phone video and game video.
- **Takes:** record each segment separately; 3 takes of the gameplay segment, then keep the best.
- **Editing:** any free editor (DaVinci Resolve, Kdenlive, CapCut). Captions in English for the
  main audience; a Spanish-subtitled version is cheap to add later.
