# Free-to-distribute song candidates for kan!'s first levels

Research-only survey of songs whose **composition AND audio** are free to
distribute, so kan! can ship a few levels everyone may legally download and
share. Owner: KairoPhos. Research date: 2026-11-09. Every licence statement
below was read off the source page given in the same cell; anything not
verified on a source page is marked **uncertain**.

## How to use this list

- **The licence rule.** Both the **composition** (tune; for hymns also the
  text) and the **audio** must be free to distribute: public domain, CC0,
  CC BY, or CC BY-SA. **NC/ND licences are excluded.** A public-domain
  melody in a copyrighted recording or a copyrighted modern arrangement is
  NOT usable.
- **Two ways to get a usable companion (the level's backing audio):**
  (a) a recording explicitly released PD/CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA (check each
  file's own licence), or (b) **kan! renders its own companion** from a
  public-domain score/MIDI. Route (b) is always safe for the audio and is
  the default for rows marked "route (b)".
- **Attribution.** CC BY and CC BY-SA need a visible credit; PD items ask
  for courtesy credit where the source page requests it. Suggested credit
  strings are given per row; put them in
  `charts/official/<song>/CREDITS.md` (provenance + licence per asset, per
  `assets/README.md` convention). Template:
  `<Title> (<origin/composer>) — <performer/recording>, via <source>, <licence>. Included in the kan! level pack (KairoPhos). Melody only; no lyrics.`
- **Share-alike hygiene.** CC BY-SA recordings carry share-alike on the
  *recording*; keep recordings in a separate `audio/` folder with their own
  licence file so the level pack's own licence is not entangled.
- **Ocarina reference.** 12-hole alto C, range **A4–F6** (concert pitch).
  Melodies may be transposed; the "range · key" column gives the melody's
  approximate span and a suggested key/octave for alto C. "✓" = fits in
  ~1.5 octaves comfortably; "≈" = borderline, verify against the charted
  melody. **All ranges are estimates of the plain melody line** (no
  ornaments, standard setting) — re-measure when the chart is authored.
- **Difficulty 1–5** assumes calm pacing (kan! is calm by design): 1 = slow
  and stepwise, 5 = wide leaps, ornaments, or brisk rhythm even when
  slowed.
- **Jurisdiction watch.** US: published before 1929 = PD. EU/UK: life+70.
  Mexico: **life+100**. Japan: works of authors who died before 1968 are PD
  in Japan. A song can be PD in one country and in copyright in another —
  the per-row notes say where that matters.
- **Verification standard.** URLs below were fetched during research
  (Commons `extmetadata`, IMSLP per-file copyright fields, Wayback captures
  where live sites were unreachable). Re-verify at import time; licences can
  change and Commons uploads can be deleted or re-licensed.

## Top 10 to make first ⭐

Balanced for culture variety, easy-to-moderate difficulty, and the
strongest licence evidence. Priority (owner 2026-11-09): Japanese and
Chinese folk and Andean melodies get most of the slots; at least 3 from
Latin America and at least 2 hymns.

1. ⭐ **Sakura Sakura** — Japan, traditional (see table)
2. ⭐ **Kōjō no Tsuki** — Japan, Rentarō Taki 1901
3. ⭐ **Furusato** — Japan, Teiichi Okano 1914
4. ⭐ **Hamabe no Uta** — Japan, Narita Tamesaburō 1916
5. ⭐ **Mo Li Hua (Jasmine Flower)** — China, traditional
6. ⭐ **Ojos Azules** — Peru, traditional huayno
7. ⭐ **El Choclo** — Argentina, Ángel Villoldo 1903
8. ⭐ **Xicochi** — Mexico (Puebla), Gaspar Fernández c. 1601
9. ⭐ **Amazing Grace** — hymn, tune NEW BRITAIN 1829
10. ⭐ **Silent Night** — hymn, Mohr/Gruber 1818

Culture spread: Japan ×4, China ×1, Peru ×1, Argentina ×1, Mexico ×1,
US/UK hymn ×1, Austrian hymn ×1. Latin America/Spain count: 3 ✓. Hymns: 2 ✓.

## Watch-outs

1. **Happy Birthday to You** — now **PD in the US and EU**. Melody from "Good Morning to All" (Mildred J. Hill **d. 1946**, publ. 1893); the 2016 US federal ruling invalidated Warner/Chappell's claim (no other claim to copyright); EU term expired **1 Jan 2017**. UK and Australia follow life+70 → also PD since 2017 (**reasoned, not directly quoted — verify**). Still: individual modern arrangements/recordings remain copyrighted. Safe to chart the melody, but re-verify the target countries before shipping.
2. **National anthems** — compositions usually PD (The Star-Spangled Banner 1814; God Save the King 1744; La Marseillaise 1792), but **modern arrangements are not**, and some anthems are modern works. Also cultural/political sensitivity: consider carefully before turning an anthem into a game level.
3. **PD melody ≠ PD recording.** Every audio companion above is separately licensed. A public-domain composition never frees someone's recording of it (e.g. famous "Amazing Grace" or "El Cóndor Pasa" recordings).
4. **"Traditional" songs with copyrighted arrangements** — always chart the traditional melody, never the published arrangement: S&G on Scarborough Fair / El Cóndor Pasa; Nyberg's 1984 Siyahamba; Obradors's El Vito (1941); Llobet's guitar figuration on El testament d'Amèlia; the 1947 harmonization of Kangding Love Song; modern settings of Mo Li Hua; Miyagi-school arrangements of Japanese tunes.
5. **Lyrics vs melody** — kan! needs only the melody and ships **no lyrics**. That rescues some cases (Morning Has Broken: tune BUNESSAN is PD although Farjeon's 1931 words are not; O mio babbino caro: melody PD but Forzano's libretto is © in the EU until 2040). But when the *melody itself* is modern (Akatombo), melody-only use does not help.
6. **Jurisdiction splits worth remembering** — Mexico **life+100** (Cielito Lindo until 2058; El Choclo lyrics; El Cóndor Pasa until 2043); US 95-year terms (El Cóndor Pasa's 1933 registration → 2029; El Vito's Obradors setting → 2037; White Christmas → 2038); **Japan's pre-1968 rule** makes Akatombo PD in Japan while the EU keeps it until 2036.
7. **Source-site caveats** — **FreePD.com is permanently closed (2008–2025)**, unusable. **Musopen** has no blanket licence: uploads are only "represented to be PD" (FAQ via Wayback, 2024-01-03 capture) — verify each file. **hymnswithoutwords.com** downloads are church-use-only; only the specific files Irwin uploaded to Commons under CC BY-SA are freely licensed. **openhymnal.org**'s TLS was broken at research time (pages verified via Wayback). **IMSLP** mixes NC/ND files in per page — check each file's copyright field. Commons tags like "PD-old-auto" come with an on-page disclaimer that the representation "may be incorrect".
8. **Share-alike hygiene** — CC BY-SA recordings: keep in a separate `audio/` folder with their own licence file so the level pack's own licence isn't entangled (share-alike bites adaptations of the recording, not the whole pack, but keep the boundary explicit in `CREDITS.md`).
9. **Poisoned uploads** — the Tōryanse "CC0" game rip shows that a licence tag can be wrong; sanity-check every file's author/credit fields.

## Do not use (verified or strongly indicated traps)

**20th-century worship / hymn traps**
- **How Great Thou Art** — English text Stuart K. Hine 1949 (+ 1953 verses), Hine 1899–1989 → © until 2060 (life+70); US renewed ©. Manna Music. Do not ship. (The 1885 Swedish poem by Boberg is PD — but everyone sings Hine's text.)
- **Morning Has Broken** — words Eleanor Farjeon 1931 (d. 1965) → © EU until 2036, US until 2027. Tune BUNESSAN is PD, but the well-known song is the words+Cat Stevens arrangement.
- **Great Is Thy Faithfulness** — PD in the US only (1923 pub, PD since 2019; Open Hymnal added it 2025); © in the EU: Chisholm d. 1960 → 2031. Not worldwide-free.
- **In Christ Alone** (Getty/Townend 2001) — ©, publishers control alterations.
- **Lord of the Dance** — Sydney Carter, published/copyrighted **1963** (d. 2004 → 2075). (The tune derives from PD Simple Gifts; the song is not free.)
- **All Things Bright and Beautiful** — the words are PD (Alexander 1848) but the tune **ROYAL OAK** (Martin Shaw 1915/1916, d. 1958) is © in the EU until 2028.
- **Shout to the Lord** (Darlene Zschech, 1993) — ©.

**Latin American traps**
- **Guantanamera** — Joseíto Fernández (d. 1979) + Seeger/Weavers 1961 arrangement. © everywhere relevant. (Only José Martí's 1891 poem is PD.)
- **Bésame Mucho** — Consuelo Velázquez (d. 2005) → © until 2075/2105.
- **Cucurrucucú Paloma** — Tomás Méndez (d. 1995).
- **Volver** — Gardel/Le Pera (both d. 1935): PD in Argentina/EU but **US until 2031** (1935 publication) — exclude from a global pack.
- **La Pollera Colorá** — Madera/Choperena, 1960/62; rights actively enforced (Colombia, life+80).
- **Malaika** — 20th-century composition with contested authorship (Adam Salim vs Fadhili William, d. 2001) and live royalty claimants.
- **Valicha** — Miguel Ángel Hurtado Delgado (**d. 2004**), composed 1940s → **NOT PD**. (Owner-flagged trap confirmed.)
- **Cielito Lindo** — Mendoza y Cortés d. 1957: PD US/Canada, © EU until 2028, © Mexico until 2058. Not for a worldwide pack.
- **Las Mañanitas** — competing authorship claims (Ponce d. 1948 / Esparza Oteo d. 1950 / Manzo Salceda) + SACM/Peer registration. Drop.

**Asian traps / flags**
- **Liu Yang River (浏阳河)** — composed **1951**, Tang Biguang (1922–2013) — **NOT PD**. Modern work, not a folk song. (Owner asked for the check: this is the answer.)
- **Gao Shan Qing (高山青)** — theme of the 1949 film *Ali Shan Feng Yun* (lyrics Deng Yuping d. 1985; music Zhang Che d. 2002) — **NOT PD**. Frequently mistaken for a Gaoshan folk song — exactly the "modern attributed version" to exclude.
- **Ue o Muite Arukō ("Sukiyaki")** — Hachidai Nakamura (1931–1992) / Rokusuke Ei (1933–2016), 1961 — © to 2063/2087. (Correction to common listings: not "Hattori/Kosaka".)
- **Kangding Love Song / Xiao He Tang Shui** — traditional cores OK, but the famous versions carry 1946–47 collection/arrangement claims → use own arrangement only, or defer (marked uncertain above).
- **The Lion Sleeps Tonight / Mbube** — Solomon Linda 1939 (d. 1962) — famous multi-party rights saga.

**Children's / Christmas traps**
- **Kookaburra** — Marion Sinclair (d. 1988) — © in Australia until 2038 (Larrikin v EMI).
- **The Wheels on the Bus** — Verna Hills, first published Dec 1937 (d. 1990) — © until at least 2033.
- **Waltzing Matilda** — murky chain of ownership (Paterson/Cowan/Carl Fischer claims) — avoid.
- **Feliz Navidad** (1970), **Jingle Bell Rock** (1957), **Rudolph** (1949), **White Christmas** (1942; US until 2038), **Little Drummer Boy** (1941/58), **Mary's Boy Child** (1956), **Do They Know It's Christmas?** (1984) — all ©.
